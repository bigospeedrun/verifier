\begin{abstract}
We prove, conditional on the cited OpenAI spatial-transfer and finite-input
results, that the periodic spin-one Heisenberg chain has a unique ground
state and $\gap_L>303/2000=0.1515$ for every integer $L\ge24$, at coupling
$J=1$, including odd lengths. New quarter-turn thermal traces resolve
the octahedral representation into four lists. Conjugacy of the physical
half-turns supplies an additional moment identity and effective spatial
multiplicities $1,4,5,3$. Eight full-ray filters, 117 full-interval moment
polynomials, and a newly contracted 84-site trial sharpen the input to
the retained recurrence. The exact certificate has 381 named checks,
39 individually checked main-range lengths, seven finite intervals,
and an infinite tail. Smaller bounds are $0.063$ for all integer $L\ge18$,
$0.119$ for $L\ge20$, and $0.145$ for $L\ge22$.
Earlier proofs and their records are retained. The work is unreviewed;
priority, optimality, a formal proof, and an independent proof of the
upstream analytic premises are not claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and dependencies}
Consider the periodic spin-one Heisenberg Hamiltonian
\[
 H_L=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\mathbf S_j\cdot\mathbf S_{j+1},
 \qquad \mathbf S_L=\mathbf S_0,
\]
with coupling $J=1$ and integer $L\ge4$. Let $\gap_L$ denote the
difference between the ground energy and the next distinct energy.
Set $Z_L(b)=\Tr e^{-b(H_L+aLI)}$, where $a=700741/500000$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:purities}
 S(n,b)=\frac{Z_{2n}(b)}{Z_n(b)^2},\qquad
 T(n,b)=\frac{Z_n(2b)}{Z_n(b)^2}.
\end{equation}
The shift cancels from both ratios. Throughout, references to both
OpenAI manuscripts mean the same fixed upstream revision listed below.
Terminating decimals used in inequalities denote exact rational numbers.
The physical purity $T(n,b)$ is defined for every integer $n\ge4$.
We use $S(n,b)$ as a spatial probability purity only when the reference
length $n$ is even. Signed spatial traces, in contrast, are available
at odd lengths too.

\subsection{Sharper enclosures and simultaneous moment certificates}

 \frac{s_n(r-E_n)^2}{Q_*^2}\le Z_n(b_*,g)

Next let $d\nu_A=\sum_{\mu\in A}\mu^4\delta_\mu$ for $A=J,N$.

 A_m\le\sum_{j=0}^8c_j A_{j+4}.

\subsection{A sector residual bound and the initial state}
Suppose an even reference order $m$ has total mass at most $D$,
$I$ mass at most $A$, and $J=I+2O$ mass at most $B$, with
$0<A\le B\le D$. A unique largest moment weight $s$ must be in $I$,
since the other lists occur two or three times. After removing it,
write $x$ for the $I$ residual mass, $y$ for the twice-counted $O$ mass,
and $z$ for the three-times-counted $N$ mass. Then
\[
 x\le A-s,\quad x+y\le B-s,\quad x+y+z\le D-s.
\]
For $r\ge1$, the residual moment of relative order $r$ is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sector-R}
 \mathcal R_r(s;D,A,B)=
 \max_{(x,y)\in\mathcal V_s}
 \{x^r+2(y/2)^r+3((D-s-x-y)/3)^r\},
\end{equation}
where
\[
 \mathcal V_s=\{(0,0),(A-s,0),(A-s,B-A),(0,B-s)\}.
\]
Indeed the uncapped moment inequality applies within each list.
Increasing $z$ to the allowed remaining mass only increases its power.
The resulting function of $(x,y)$ is convex on the polygon defined by
the first two constraints, whose four vertices are displayed.
This also proves the countable-list case by absolute summability.

For $D/2<s\le A$, each vertex expression in
$s^r+\mathcal R_r(s)$ is increasing in $s$ (strictly if $r>1$).
Its derivative compares $s^{r-1}$ with a residual term no larger than
$(D-s)^{r-1}$. Thus its maximum is increasing too. For $s\ge v>D/2$,
$\mathcal R_r(s)\le\mathcal R_r(v)$: shrinking the allowed residual
polygon cannot increase the original optimization. These observations
justify both the leading-weight comparison and the residual estimate.

Write $M_J(m),M_N(m)$ for the certified upper bounds and $m_N(m)$ for
the lower bound at $b_*$. For $t\ge1$, physical positivity gives
$Z_m(tb_*,g)\le Z_m(b_*,g)^t$ for each twist $g$.
These are partition functions of twisted self-adjoint Hamiltonians,
not signed Gibbs expectations. Since at even order
$Z(P)=J-N$ and $Z(C)=I-O\le J$, valid caps at $tb_*$ are
\begin{align*}
 D&=(M_J(m)+3M_N(m))^t,&
 E_P&=(M_J(m)-m_N(m))^t, &E_C&=M_J(m)^t,\\
 A&=(D+3E_P+8E_C)/12,& B&=(D+3E_P)/4.
\end{align*}
All powers and caps in the implementation are enclosed outward.
The 120-site trial implies $Z_{120}(tb_*)>1$.
If $2(D/2)^{120/m}<1$, capped mass therefore forces a unique leading
moment weight above $D/2$. It lies in $I$. A candidate $v$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sector-v}
 D/2<v\le\min(1,A),\qquad
 v^{120/m}+\mathcal R_{120/m}(v;D,A,B)<1
\end{equation}
forces $\lambda(tb_*)^m>v$. The dominant spatial eigenvalue is positive:
if it were negative, the absolutely convergent signed trace at large
odd lengths would be negative, contradicting the positive physical
partition function. No odd-chain gap is presumed.

For the main seed take $m=26$, $t=11/5$, and even reference $n=44$.
At order $n$, divide the residual bounds by $v^{n/m}$ to obtain
\begin{align*}
 i&\ge (A-v)^{n/m}/v^{n/m},\\
 j&\ge\max\{2((B-v)/2)^{n/m},
                (A-v)^{n/m}+2((B-A)/2)^{n/m}\}/v^{n/m},\\
 r&\ge\mathcal R_{n/m}(v;D,A,B)/v^{n/m}.
\end{align*}
Thus $i,j,r$ bound, respectively, the normalized $I$ residual,
$J$ residual, and full residual at $(n,b)=(44,539/20)$.

The same published $26$-dimensional integer matrix triple is newly
contracted at length $88$. Put $V_{88}=\Tr(T^{88})$ and
$U_{88}=\Tr(T^{86}O)$ as in \cite[Appendix C]{upstream}.
The recorded integers satisfy
\[

 \qquad U_{88}/V_{88}\simeq-1.4014821054306783.
\]
Hence $Z_{88}(b)>1$ for all $b>0$. The new contraction is independently
reconstructed from the published entry recipe; a four-site direct

Full integers are in \path{independent_results/trial88.json};

If $Z_{88}(b_*)\le M$, its largest physical Boltzmann weight exceeds

\[
 T(88,tb_*)\ge[1+(M-1)^t]^{-2}.
\]
This supplies the physical defect $q$. No assumed physical excitation

\subsection{Retaining the sectors through doubling}
At even $(n,b)$, let the positive leading eigenvalue be $\lambda$.
Suppose $i\le j\le r$ are upper bounds for the three normalized residual
masses just defined, and $1-T(2n,b)\le q$. Then
\[
 Z_n(b)\le\lambda^n(1+r),\quad
 Z_n(b,P)\le\lambda^n(1+j),\quad
 Z_n(b,C)\le\lambda^n(1+i).
\]
Squaring each positive physical partition function bounds its value
at $2b$. In units $\lambda^{2n}$ the new caps are
\begin{align*}
 D&=(1+r)^2=1+d_D,\\
 B&=[(1+r)^2+3(1+j)^2]/4=1+d_J,\\
 A&=[(1+r)^2+3(1+j)^2+8(1+i)^2]/12=1+d_I.
\end{align*}
On the other hand, the sum of squares of the new $n$th-moment weights is
\[
 \frac{Z_{2n}(2b)}{\lambda^{4n}}
 =T(2n,b)\left(\frac{Z_{2n}(b)}{\lambda^{2n}}\right)^2
 \ge1-q.
\]
When $D^2/2<1-q$, there is again a unique leading weight above $D/2$.
For $h>0$, let $v=1-h>D/2$ and define
\begin{align*}
 R(h)=\max\{& (d_D+h)^2/3,\ (d_I+h)^2+(d_D-d_I)^2/3,\\
            & (d_I+h)^2+(d_J-d_I)^2/2+(d_D-d_J)^2/3,\\
            & (d_J+h)^2/2+(d_D-d_J)^2/3\}.
\end{align*}
This is \eqref{eq:sector-R} at relative order two. The strict comparison
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sector-h}
 q+R(h)<2h-h^2
\end{equation}
forces $\lambda(2b)^n/\lambda(b)^{2n}>v$, by the same increasing-function
argument as \eqref{eq:sector-v}. At the new reference $(2n,2b)$, we may use
\begin{align*}
 i_+&\ge(d_I+h)^2/v^2,\\
 j_+&\ge\max\{(d_J+h)^2/2,(d_I+h)^2+(d_J-d_I)^2/2\}/v^2,\\
 r_+&\ge R(h)/v^2,\qquad p_+=1-(1+r_+)^{-2},\\
 q_+&\ge f\bigl(1-(1-q)^2(1-p_+)\bigr),\qquad
 f(u)=u^2/[2(1-u)^2].
\end{align*}
The $r_+$ estimate implies $S(2n,2b)\ge(1+r_+)^{-2}$.
The last line follows from the original partition-function cancellation
and physical squaring proof: $T(4n,b)\ge(1-q)^2(1-p_+)$, followed by
temperature doubling. Thus only the spatial part of the update is
replaced. Upward rounding of $i_+,j_+,r_+,q_+$ preserves all bounds.

\subsection{A tighter filter and partition-function upper bounds}
Put $b_*=49/4$. The spatial spectrum consists of a $J$ sector and three
identical $N$ sectors. Let $j_k$ be the published center for $J_k$,
$4\le k\le12$, with absolute error at most
$\epsilon=30\times10^{-6}$. These centers are
$(Z_k+3Z_k(P))/4$ from the thermal table in
\cite[Lemma 5.1]{upstream}, and are recorded in the checker.
Choose
\begin{align*}
 P(x)&=12328+32348x-499897x^2-339164x^3+1787190x^4,\\
 F(x)&=x^4P(x)^2=\sum_{k=4}^{12}f_kx^k,\qquad
 B_F=\sum_{k=4}^{12}(f_kj_k+\epsilon|f_k|),\\

\end{align*}
Exact rational comparisons give $F(U),F(-U)>B_F$.
Every coefficient of $P(U+y)$ and $P(-U-y)$ is positive, so both
$F(U+y)$ and $F(-U-y)$ increase for $y\ge0$.
Since $F\ge0$ on the real line and the summed filter is at most $B_F$,
every $J$ eigenvalue has modulus less than $U$. The former cap was
$1.00139$. We retain the published cap $V$ in the $N$ sectors and the
published twelfth-moment bounds
\[
 m_J=1.0657205,\qquad m_N=.2783155.
\]

where $h<m<2h$, the capped-mass lemma gives
$\sum_i t_i^r\le h^r+(m-h)^r$ for every real $r\ge1$.
Applying this with $t_i=|\mu_i|^{12}$ separately in each sector yields,
for every even integer $k\ge12$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-Z}
 Z_k(b_*)\le U^k+(m_J-U^{12})^{k/12}
       +3\bigl[V^k+(m_N-V^{12})^{k/12}\bigr].
\end{equation}
Using the cap consistently in both the leading term and the remaining
mass avoids maximizing these two terms separately.
The exact certificate proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-Zvalues}
 Z_{32}(b_*)<B_{32}:=1.08612266,\qquad
 Z_{120}(b_*)<B_{120}:=1.175802453.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Dominant-eigenvalue lower bounds and new seeds}
The original argument gives a positive dominant $J$ eigenvalue
$\lambda(b_*)>.999$. Removing it and using the original twelfth-moment
bounds gives the residual upper bound
\[
 A_{120}=(m_J-.999^{12})^{10}
          +3\bigl[V^{120}+(m_N-V^{12})^{10}\bigr].
\]
The trial input implies $Z_{120}(b)>1$ at every $b>0$.
With $\ell=.999999998$, the checker verifies
$\ell^{120}+A_{120}<1$. Hence $\lambda(b_*)>\ell$.
Removing this stronger leading contribution gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-short-tail}
 R_{b_*}(32)<.04236466,\qquad \rho_{b_*}<.872001,
\end{equation}
where $R_b(k)$ is the residual absolute moment normalized by the
positive dominant eigenvalue, and $\rho_b$ is the largest residual
absolute eigenvalue ratio. For the ratio bound, use
$m_J-\ell^{12}<V^{12}$ and $V/\ell<.872001$.

At the doubled temperature, physical purity gives
$Z_{32}(2b_*)\le Z_{32}(b_*)^2<M:=B_{32}^2$.
Let $\ell_2=.9999865$ and $v=\ell_2^{32}$.
The rational certificate proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-dominance-check}
 M/2<v<M,\qquad (M-v)^{15}<(1-\ell_2^{120})^4.
\end{equation}
If every $|\mu_i(2b_*)|^{32}$ were at most $v$, the capped-mass lemma
with exponent $120/32=15/4$ would imply
\[
 1<Z_{120}(2b_*)\le v^{15/4}+(M-v)^{15/4}<1,
\]
a contradiction. Thus a unique eigenvalue has modulus exceeding
$\ell_2$; uniqueness follows from $2v>M$.
It is positive: a unique negative dominant eigenvalue would make the
signed trace negative at sufficiently large odd lengths, contradicting
the positivity of the physical partition function. Absolute convergence
at order $32$ justifies domination of the residual at those lengths.
This argument assumes no odd-chain spectral gap.

The further rational comparison
\[

We first record the concentration bound on the domain needed below.
The companion states its rational version for defects at most $.1$;
its elementary proof extends to all defects below $1/2$.
\begin{lemma}[Concentration]\label{lem:concentration}
Let $(p_i)$ be a finite or countable probability list with
$\sum_i p_i^2\ge1-z$, where $0\le z<1/2$.
Its largest weight $p_0$ is unique, and
\[
 \frac{\sum_{i\ne0}p_i}{p_0}\le d(z),\qquad
 d(z)=\frac{z}{2-3z}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A summable nonzero nonnegative list attains its maximum.
Since $\sum_i p_i^2\le p_0$, one has $p_0\ge1-z>1/2$.
Put $x=1-p_0\le z$. Then
$2x(1-x)\le1-\sum_i p_i^2\le z$, so
$x\le z/[2(1-x)]\le z/[2(1-z)]$.
Applying the increasing function $x\mapsto x/(1-x)$ gives the result;
the displayed upper bound for $x$ is below one when $z<1/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:all-lengths}]
Reindex the companion's scales $j\ge2$ by $k=j-2\ge0$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-scales}
 n_k=120\,2^k,\qquad \beta_k=49\,2^k,\qquad
 u_k=\frac16\left(\frac{39}{125}\right)^{2^k}.
\end{equation}
Thus $u_0=13/250$ and $u_{k+1}=6u_k^2\le u_k$.
Write $n=n_k$, $\beta=\beta_k$, $u=u_k$, and $v=u_{k+1}$.
The cited propositions supply real spatial eigenvalues $\mu_i(b)$ with
absolutely convergent moments and
\[
 Z_L(b)=\sum_i\mu_i(b)^L\qquad(\text{every integer }L\ge4).
\]
At $b=\beta,2\beta$, their respective unique eigenvalues of maximal
modulus are positive; denote these by $\lambda,\lambda'$.
With $c=\lambda'/\lambda^2$, the companion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-inputs}
 S(n,\beta)\ge1-u,\quad T(2n,\beta)\ge1-u,\quad
 0<c\le1,\quad c^{2n}\ge(1-u)(1-v).
\end{equation}
Positivity of the leading eigenvalue does not assume an odd-chain gap:
the companion proves it from positivity of physical partition functions
and dominance in the signed trace at arbitrarily large odd lengths.

The necessary control at the \emph{same} reference length $n$ and the
next temperature follows by partition-function cancellation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-same-n}
 S(n,2\beta)=\frac{S(n,\beta)^2T(2n,\beta)}{T(n,\beta)^2}
 \ge(1-u)^3\ge1-3u.
\end{equation}
Here $T(n,\beta)\le1$. Because $n$ is even, the two spatial lists at
this length are probability lists. Lemma~\ref{lem:concentration},
applicable since $3u\le39/250<1/2$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-tails}
 \sum_{i\ne0}\left|\frac{\mu_i(\beta)}{\lambda}\right|^n\le d(u),
 \qquad
 \sum_{i\ne0}\left|\frac{\mu_i(2\beta)}{\lambda'}\right|^n\le d(3u).
\end{equation}
Each absolute eigenvalue ratio is at most one. Increasing its exponent
from $n$ to any integer $L\ge n$ therefore decreases every summand.
Absolute convergence and the triangle inequality yield
\[
 Z_L(\beta)=\lambda^L(1+\epsilon_L),\quad |\epsilon_L|\le d(u),
 \qquad
 Z_L(2\beta)=(\lambda')^L(1+\epsilon'_L),\quad
 |\epsilon'_L|\le d(3u).
\]
These estimates allow negative nondominant eigenvalues. No probability
interpretation of an odd spatial moment is used. In particular,
\eqref{eq:odd-same-n} is essential: a higher-moment upper bound alone
cannot bound a lower moment by taking a root.

For every integer $L\in[n,2n]$, $c^L\ge c^{2n}$, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-K}
 T(L,\beta)\ge K(u):=
 \frac{(1-u)(1-6u^2)(1-d(3u))}{(1+d(u))^2}
 \ge1-\frac92u.
\end{equation}
To prove the last inequality on the entire interval $0\le u\le13/250$,
use the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-polynomial}
 K(u)-\left(1-\frac92u\right)=
 \frac{u\bigl[4-60u+243u^2(1-2u)+324u^4\bigr]}
      {2(2-9u)(1-u)}\ge0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $4-60u\ge22/25>0$, $1-2u>0$, and both denominator factors
are positive on that interval. In particular,
$T(L,\beta)\ge383/500>1/2$.

Let $w_0$ be the largest physical Gibbs weight, counting eigenvectors
with multiplicity. Since $w_0\ge T(L,\beta)>1/2$, the ground state is
unique. For an eigenvector of the next distinct energy,
\begin{align*}
 e^{-\beta_k\gap_L}
 &=\frac{w_1}{w_0}
 \le\frac{(9/2)u_k}{1-(9/2)u_k}\\
 &\le\frac{375}{383}\left(\frac{39}{125}\right)^{2^k}
 <\left(\frac{39}{125}\right)^{2^k}.
\end{align*}
Taking logarithms and dividing by $\beta_k=49\,2^k$ proves the claimed
strict bound on each interval $[120\,2^k,240\,2^k]$. These intervals
cover all integer $L\ge120$. The rounded consequence follows from the
exact exponential comparison described in \eqref{eq:expupper}.
The liminf inequality follows from the uniform finite-length bound.
\end{proof}

        \text{for every even }L\ge2304.}
\]
Consequently $\liminf_{L\to\infty,\,L\ {\rm even}}\gap_L\ge47/10000$.
For any positive exchange coupling $J$, multiply these bounds by $J$.
\end{theorem}
The argument below rederives the needed bootstrap steps of
\cite[Lemma 4.2 and Proposition 4.3]{upstream}. It does not establish
the two inputs independently of the upstream analytic argument.

\section{Purity estimates and gap extraction}
For an even reference length $n\ge4$, both quantities in
\eqref{eq:purities} are purities of probability
distributions, so they belong to $(0,1]$. For spatial purity use
$|\lambda_i(b)|^n/Z_n(b)$; for physical purity use the Gibbs weights,
counting each eigenvector with multiplicity. If $P=\sum_i w_i^2$,
the normalized squares have purity $P'$ satisfying
\[
 1-P'=\frac{2\sum_{i<k}w_i^2w_k^2}{P^2}
 \le\frac{2(\sum_{i<k}w_iw_k)^2}{P^2}
 =f(1-P),\qquad f(x)=\frac{x^2}{2(1-x)^2}.
\]
Here $f$ is increasing on $[0,1)$.

\begin{lemma}[Rounded update]\label{lem:update}
For even $n\ge4$ and $b>0$, if $1-S(n,b)\le p$ and
$1-T(2n,b)\le q$, with $p,q\in[0,1)$,
any $p_+,q_+\in[0,1)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:update}
 p_+\ge f\bigl(1-(1-p)^2(1-q)\bigr),\qquad
 q_+\ge f\bigl(1-(1-q)^2(1-p_+)\bigr)
\end{equation}
bound $1-S(2n,2b)$ and $1-T(4n,2b)$ respectively.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Cancellation of partition functions gives
\[
 S(n,2b)=\frac{S(n,b)^2T(2n,b)}{T(n,b)^2},\qquad
 T(4n,b)=\frac{T(2n,b)^2S(2n,2b)}{S(2n,b)^2}.
\]
The denominators are at most one. Apply the probability-squaring
inequality first in the spatial variable and then in the temperature
variable. Use the already rounded $p_+$ in the second application.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Intermediate lengths]\label{lem:interval}
Under the same hypotheses, put $K=(1-p)^2(1-q)$.
If $K>1/2$, every even $L\in[n,2n]$ has a unique ground state and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intervalgap}
 \gap_L\ge\frac1b\log\frac{K}{1-K}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The monotonicity of sequence $\ell^r$ norms, applied to the spatial
moments at $b$ and $2b$, yields
\[
 Z_L(b)\le Z_n(b)^{L/n},\qquad
 Z_L(2b)\ge Z_{2n}(2b)^{L/(2n)}.
\]
Hence
\[
 T(L,b)\ge[T(2n,b)S(n,b)^2]^{L/(2n)}\ge K.
\]
The last step uses a base in $(0,1]$ and an exponent at most one.
The largest Gibbs weight $w_0$ is at least the purity, because
$\sum_iw_i^2\le(\max_iw_i)\sum_iw_i$. Thus $w_0>1/2$, excluding
ground-state degeneracy. For one eigenvector at the next energy,
\[
 e^{-b\gap_L}=\frac{w_1}{w_0}\le\frac{1-w_0}{w_0}
 \le\frac{1-K}{K}.
\]
Taking logarithms proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

For $j=0,\ldots,4$, set $K_j=(1-p_j)^2(1-q_j)$.
The certificate verifies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expcheck}
 K_j>\tfrac12,\qquad
 e^{(47/10000)b_j}<\frac{K_j}{1-K_j}.
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:interval} therefore proves the strict target bound
throughout $2304\le L\le73728$, restricted to even lengths.

For clarity, the exponential comparisons use no numerical transcendental
evaluation. Given rational $x\ge0$, choose
$m=4\lfloor x\rfloor+64$. Since $\rho=x/(m+2)<1$, the Taylor
series and the ratio bound for its remaining terms imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expupper}
 e^x\le E_m(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{x^k}{k!}
 +\frac{x^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}\frac{1}{1-x/(m+2)}.
\end{equation}
Every term on the right is rational. The script verifies the stronger
inequality obtained by replacing $e^x$ with $E_m(x)$ in
\eqref{eq:expcheck}.

The final row controls all remaining lengths. Let
\[
 u=1.10\times10^{-52},\quad C=5,\quad r=Cu,\qquad
 G(v)=\frac12\bigl((1-v)^{-1}+(1-v)^{-2}+(1-v)^{-3}\bigr)^2.
\]
Exact checks give $0<u<1/6$, $G(u)<C$, $r<1$, and
$C(1-3u)>3$. Define $u_k=C^{-1}r^{2^k}$ for $k\ge0$.
Both defects at $(2^k n_5,2^k b_5)$ are bounded by $u_k$:
indeed, $u_{k+1}=Cu_k^2<u_k$, and
\[
 f\bigl(1-(1-u_k)^3\bigr)=G(u_k)u_k^2\le Cu_k^2.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:update} then applies to both coordinates by induction.
For even $L\in[2^k n_5,2^{k+1}n_5]$, the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:interval} gives $w_0\ge(1-u_k)^3\ge1-3u_k$.
Consequently
\[
 e^{-(2^k b_5)\gap_L}
 \le\frac{3u_k}{1-3u_k}
 \le\frac{3}{C(1-3u)}r^{2^k}<r^{2^k}.
\]
These intervals cover every even $L\ge n_5$, proving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
 \gap_L>\frac{\log(1/(Cu))}{b_5}
 =\frac{\log(10^{53}/55)}{25088}
 =0.004704626783460297\ldots>\frac{47}{10000}.
\end{equation}
The final strict comparison is certified by
$E_m((47/10000)25088)<10^{53}/55$, again over the rationals.
Together with the five finite intervals this proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:legacy}. A strict bound for each finite length yields
the stated non-strict bound on the liminf.
